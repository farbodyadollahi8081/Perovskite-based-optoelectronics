# Perovskite Nanowire Photodetector Fabrication – HKUST Internship (Jul–Oct 2025)
This repository documents my research work conducted as a Visiting Research Intern at the Functional and Advanced Nanostructures Laboratory (FAN Lab), Hong Kong University of Science and Technology (HKUST) under the supervision of Prof. Zhiyong Fan, Dr. Swapnadeep Poddar, and Shivam Kumar.

The project focused on the fabrication and optimization of perovskite-based photodetectors using anodic aluminum oxide (AAO) templates. Two fabrication routes were developed and compared:

- Chemical Vapor Deposition (CVD) for MAPbI₃ nanowires.
- Spin Coating for MAPbBr₃ nanowires.

Both approaches utilized porous anodic alumina (PAA) as a self-ordered nanostructured template. Key processes included Pb electrodeposition and barrier layer thinning, which ensured effective electron transport through the nanowire–substrate interface. The photodetector devices were completed by ITO sputtering, forming transparent conductive contacts.

The repository includes:

- Process documentation (anodization, CVD growth, spin coating, Pb deposition, ITO sputtering).
- Experimental data, SEM images, and analysis of nanowire morphology and device performance.
- Measurement scripts and data-processing tools for probe station and optical characterization.
- Methodology drafts and progress notes summarizing experimental parameters and outcomes.

Techniques: SEM, FIB, sputtering, CVD, AAO anodization, electrochemical polishing, Pb electrodeposition, barrier thinning, probe station testing, and optical power measurements.

This work aimed to compare vapor-phase and solution-phase nanowire growth methods, evaluate their influence on structural uniformity, and optimize fabrication parameters for improved device responsivity and reproducibility.
